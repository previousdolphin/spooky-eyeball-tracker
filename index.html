01 — CATALOGUE ENTRY

The Specimen

Spooky Eyeball Tracker began as a simple question for a haunted attraction client: what if a prop could tell you were there before you noticed it? The answer is this eye — a single, impossibly wet-looking orb that hangs in empty space, its sclera laced with hand-mapped veins, its lower hemisphere sagging into drips as though gravity is still deciding what to do with it.

It is not scripted to glance around at random. It is built to look at something — the nearest warm, moving shape in frame — and to hold that look a beat longer than feels comfortable. The result reads less like animation and more like attention.

Everything you see is generated at runtime: the vein pattern, the drip geometry, the glossy cornea highlight. No two loads of the page paint the eye identically, the same way no two real eyes are.

02 — METHOD

How It Watches

There is no face-recognition model buried in this page — no ML weights downloaded, no biometric database. The eye uses a much blunter, older trick: find whatever in the frame is warm, bright and moving, and assume that's the nearest living thing.

  1. 01

    Optical Intake

    With your permission, the camera stream is captured directly in the browser and never transmitted anywhere. Each frame is downsampled to a tiny working image for speed.

  2. 02

    Luminance & Motion Scan

    Every pixel is scored for skin-warm color bias and for how much it changed since the last frame. Clusters that are both warm and shifting score highest — a crude but surprisingly effective proxy for "a person."

  3. 03

    Proximity Estimate

    The size and strength of the winning cluster becomes a rough proximity read — NEAR, MEDIUM, or FAR — displayed live on the telemetry panel beside the eye.

  4. 04

    Gaze Lock

    That cluster's position is projected into 3D space and the eyeball's rotation is smoothed toward it frame over frame, clamped to a believable ±35° cone, so the stare creeps rather than snaps.

03 — DATASHEET

Specimen Datasheet

SCLERA MATERIAL

Procedural membrane, clearcoat 1.0, roughness 0.28, runtime-generated radial vein mapping

GEOMETRY

96×96 segment sphere; lower hemisphere displaced into 6–8 asymmetric drip protrusions

IRIS & PUPIL

Bloodshot crimson fiber pattern, glossy corneal bulge with fresnel-style highlight

TRACKING METHOD

Local luminance + motion centroid, 2D→3D projection, clamped ±35° gaze cone, exponential smoothing

ENVIRONMENT

Dust-mote particle field, warm key + cool rim lighting, exponential fog matched to void

RESPONSE LATENCY

~90ms frame-to-gaze, interpolated so the eye never jumps, only arrives
